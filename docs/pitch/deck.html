Busy stores have a blind spot.

  • While travelling in Oslo, I met my old Domino's employer. He owns the Burger King near Aker Brygge.
  • I told him about my startup. He said his problem is not knowing why his stores make sales.
  • And not knowing what happens nearby in real time, to forecast stock. Example: the trains stop working, and sales go up.
  • I worked at his Domino's in Majorstuen. When a rush hit, I was called in.
  • Many times the rush was over by the time I arrived. That means lost sales and unhappy customers.
It is a personal and real problem.

Not only for Oslo. For everywhere.

  • Built on one real Burger King in Oslo, at Klingenberggata 5.
  • Any store with an address can get the same local picture.

Pulse watches the area for you.

  • Real-time data for one local store.
  • Events nearby.
  • Transport and disruptions.
  • Weather, and more.
  • An hourly forecast of orders, the crew it needs, and why.

What we built in 48 hours.

Model

  • LightGBM quantile models (P10, P50, P90), calibrated with split conformal prediction.
  • 21.4% WAPE on a held-out year, against 31.0% for the hour and weekday average.
  • Trees exported to TypeScript, with a parity test against Python.

Live data

  • MET Norway Locationforecast and Frost observations.
  • Entur Journey Planner, live vehicles, and 1.8 million past stop calls (SIRI-ET on BigQuery).
  • Venue programmes scraped live with context.dev.

Agents and staffing

  • Gemini on Vertex AI: an event scout with Google Search grounding, a road camera watcher, a staffing overseer.
  • Erlang C crew per hour and a set-covering shift plan (linear programming).
  • 38 tests, including Norwegian Working Environment Act rules.
Store sales are simulated on top of the real inputs until a till system is connected.

Who we sell to first.
